A research note on tokens that do not exist yet

198 protocols without a token, priced from the market that already trades.

Every protocol on the snapshot's watchlist — $50M or more of TVL and no token on record — plus every other pre-token protocol that passes the watchlist's own test. Each one is valued from a curve fitted on the 92 traded tokens in the same snapshot, the fit is tested on tokens it never saw, and each farm plan is netted to the dollar. Implied value is a model, not a promise; no token here is guaranteed to launch.

The fitted value curve

Traded tokens set the price of untraded ones.

The model, in one line

implied = TVL × exp(curve + category) × growth × maturity

The curve is ordinary least squares of log(mcap/TVL) on log(TVL) across the comps. The category term is that category's median residual off the curve, shrunk toward zero and capped at ×2 either way (nothing for a category with fewer than two traded comps). Growth and maturity are bounded, stated tilts (±25% and 0.85–1.15).

Two blind tests: the model is refit on half the comps and scored on the half it never saw. The flat column prices every held-out token at the training median — the model has to beat doing nothing.

    How this radar works

    Everything below is computed from the snapshot on every run. Nothing here is a hard-coded list of names.

    1. The board

    The watchlist is the floor: all 40 of its protocols are ranked. The radar adds every other pre-token protocol that passes the watchlist's own test — 158 more, 198 in all. Wrapped, staked and bridged products of tokens that already trade (WBTC, USDT0, the exchange bridges), exchange products and versioned pools are kept out, because a radar that ranks a wrapped token as a launch candidate is not a radar.

    2. The value model

    92 traded-token protocols in the snapshot carry both a market cap and a TVL. The model regresses log(mcap/TVL) on log(TVL) across them: big protocols trade at a smaller multiple of TVL, and the curve measures that slope instead of assuming it. Each row then gets its category's median residual off the curve (shrunk by n/(n+5), nothing for a category with fewer than two traded comps, and capped so no adjustment moves a row by more than ×2 either way — one outlier comp must not crown a protocol), a growth tilt from its 7-day change and a maturity tilt from how long it has run with no token. The 80% interval comes from the fit's residual dispersion — the market disagrees wildly about what TVL is worth, and the band says so.

    3. The blind tests

    A curve that only fits the tokens it was fitted on proves nothing. So the model is refit on half the comps and scored on the other half (split-half), and refit on the older-listed half and scored on the newer-listed half (time-split). Both tests report the median error and the share of held-out tokens priced within 2×, next to a flat-median baseline. The curve is also bootstrapped 200 times; the fitted multiple at $100M of TVL is reported with its 10th–90th percentile range.

    4. The farm verdict

    Each plan's expected gross is the implied value × 15% assumed farmer allocation × (your capital ÷ 5% of TVL assumed to be actively farming), ×1.5 for protocols listed under a year ago. The cost is the plan's own gas (setup, four weeks of weekly actions, the claim) plus a $6 time proxy. FARM means the net is $25 or more, THIN means it clears zero, SKIP means the plan costs more than it is expected to return. The assumptions are stated here so a reader can disagree with them. Note what follows: the biggest protocols are often SKIP, because a $250 position is too small a share of a multi-billion-dollar pool to earn back its gas — the most valuable launches are not always the best farms.

    How to read this entry

    Criterion 1 — the board above covers all 40 watchlist protocols and ranks 198 pre-token ones by stated implied value; the screenshots show this page as it renders from the same data the program writes. Criterion 2 — the value comes from the fitted curve in the first panel, derived from the snapshot's traded tokens, with the derivation shown per protocol in its detail view; traded tokens live in their own section below, never in the ranking. Criterion 3 — every detail view carries six farm steps with a cost and a time, and a verdict netted to the dollar. Criterion 4 — the board sorts and filters, and every row opens a detail view; two rows can be compared side by side. Criterion 5 — one palette, one type system, ledger rules throughout.

    What this radar does not do: it does not predict whether or when any token launches. “Records no token” means DefiLlama records no token for the protocol; it is not proof none exists. Implied value is this entry's own claim, never make.fun's.
